About this map

This is the world of a fan-run Worlds Adrift server, drawn live. The islands, the weather walls and the zone colours come from the game's own map data. Everything moving on top of it is happening right now on the server.

Wildlife

Manta rays circle each island. Jellyfish drift underneath by day and rise to deck height at night.

The server doesn't send us where each creature is. It sends a headcount per island and the time on its clock. Your browser then runs the same movement maths the server runs, from that clock. That's why the creatures glide instead of jumping every few seconds, and why what you see matches the game rather than approximating it.

Islands vary. Bigger ones support more animals, and populations rise and fall over time, so some islands are busy and some are nearly empty. That's the world, not a bug.

Ships

Each ship is drawn as the actual outline of the hull someone built, seen from above, pointing the way it's heading.

Positions arrive every few seconds. Between updates we move a ship along the speed the server last reported. A dashed outline means we're estimating; a solid one means that's exactly where the server put it. We stop estimating after a few seconds rather than let a guess drift.

Travellers

Players show up as plain dots. No names, no accounts, nothing that says who anyone is. The dots are given a scrambled id so a marker doesn't teleport between updates, and those ids are thrown away and remade every time the server restarts.

Islands

Click any island for what's on it: ore, trees, databanks, wildlife. Zoom in past a few kilometres and islands take on their real coastlines and names.
